# Account basics

**A HabitForge account is a single Google identity that grants access to the same data on every device.** This page covers what is in your profile, how multi-device sync works, how to sign out, how to switch between accounts, and what happens when you install HabitForge on a new device.

If you have not signed up yet, start at [Sign up](./sign-up).

## What's in your profile

Your profile stores the bare minimum needed for the app to work:

| Field | Source | Visible where |
|---|---|---|
| Display name | From your Google account | Top-right of the app, on shared content |
| Email | From your Google account | Profile page only |
| Profile picture | From your Google account | Top-right avatar |
| `uid` | Generated by Firebase on first sign-in | Internal only |
| Settings | App-managed (theme, defaults) | Settings page |
| Created at, last sign-in | App-managed | Profile page |

There is no separate HabitForge username, password, or display-name override. If you change your name in your Google account, HabitForge picks up the new name on next sign-in.

The profile document lives at `hf_users/{uid}` in Firestore. Deleting your account deletes this document. See [Privacy basics](./privacy-basics) for the exhaustive list of what is stored.

## Multi-device sync

Sign in to HabitForge on a second device — a phone, a tablet, the browser extension on your laptop — and within seconds your habits, completions, tracker logs, and journal entries are there. The sync is real-time:

- Mark a habit complete on your phone → the strength bar moves on your laptop within ~1 second.
- Add a water-tracker entry on the extension → it shows up in the dashboard on the phone immediately.

The mechanism is Firestore's real-time listeners. Every page that displays user data subscribes to the relevant Firestore collection while open and unsubscribes when you navigate away.

### What is *not* synced

- Theme preference (light / dark / system) is per-device until you set an accent color, then it syncs as part of settings.
- Reminder permissions — granting Notifications on the web does not automatically grant them on iOS / Android. Each platform's permission grant is local.
- The recently-used dashboard quick-log row is per-device.

### Sync when offline

If you complete a habit while offline, Firestore's offline cache holds the write locally. As soon as the device reconnects, the write is pushed and any server-side changes are pulled. You will see a small "syncing" indicator while the queue drains.

Conflicts (rare) — same habit completed on two devices in the same minute — are resolved by Firestore's last-writer-wins rule, but the strength change for both writes is applied additively, so you don't lose threads to a race.

## Surfaces

HabitForge has four surfaces, all sharing the same account:

| Surface | Use case | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Web** at [habitforge.aoneahsan.com](https://habitforge.aoneahsan.com) | Desktop / laptop. Installable as a PWA. | Full feature set. |
| **iOS app** | Mobile, native notifications. | Same code as web, packaged via Capacitor. |
| **Android app** | Mobile, native notifications. | Same code as web, packaged via Capacitor. |
| **Browser extension** | Quick-add and at-a-glance dashboard. | Subset of features; full web app for editing. |

Sign in once on any surface; switching to another surface lands you straight on the dashboard.

## Signing out

### On web

Top-right avatar → **Sign out**. The page reloads and lands on the marketing / login screen.

### On mobile

Profile tab → **Sign out**.

### In the browser extension

Extension popup → settings → **Sign out**.

Signing out clears the Google session token and the local Firestore cache. Your data is not deleted — sign in again and everything is back.

### Signing out everywhere

To sign out of HabitForge on every device at once, revoke the Google authorisation:

1. Go to [Google Account → Security → Third-party apps](https://myaccount.google.com/connections).
2. Find HabitForge.
3. Click **Remove access**.

Every signed-in HabitForge surface will prompt for re-authorisation on its next refresh. This is the right approach if a device is lost or stolen.

## Switching accounts

To use a different Google account on the same device:

1. Sign out of HabitForge.
2. Sign in again. The Google sign-in screen will let you pick a different account or add a new one.
3. HabitForge loads the data for the new account.

You cannot have two HabitForge accounts logged in simultaneously on the same surface — the user document is keyed to a single Google `uid`. To use two accounts on the same physical device, use two browser profiles or two Android user profiles.

## Installing on a new device

1. Sign in with the same Google account.
2. Wait a few seconds for the initial Firestore sync.
3. Done. All habits, completions, journals, and tracker logs are there.

There is no manual import / export step needed for adding a device. Export only matters if you want a copy of your data outside HabitForge — see [Privacy basics](./privacy-basics) for export.

## Optional Google Drive backup

If you opt into the [Google Drive integration](/docs/theme), HabitForge writes journal exports to a folder in your Google Drive. The folder is created on first use and named `HabitForge Backups`. The OAuth scope used is `drive.file`, which means HabitForge can only read and write files it created in your Drive — it cannot see, list, or modify your existing files.

Drive backup is per-account: when you sign in on a new device, the backup folder is the same one (your Drive's), and HabitForge will pick up where the previous device left off. Revoke the Drive scope at any time from Google Account → Security without affecting the rest of HabitForge.

## Account-level controls

The Profile page exposes:

- **Sign out** (this device only).
- **Delete account** — irreversible; removes your Firestore documents. See [Privacy basics → Delete account](./privacy-basics#delete-your-account).
- **Export my data** — downloads a JSON file of your habits, completions, journals, and tracker logs. Always available, regardless of whether Google Drive is connected.
- **Disconnect Google Drive** — revokes the `drive.file` scope (without deleting the Drive folder).
- **Theme settings** — light / dark / system, accent color, font size.

There is no "merge account" feature. Two HabitForge accounts are independent and cannot be combined.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I sign in with a different Google account by mistake?

You'll see an empty dashboard — that's the new account's empty state, not a data-loss bug. Sign out, sign in with the correct Google account, and your data reappears.

### Can I rename my account?

Update your Google account name. HabitForge mirrors it on next sign-in.

### What happens if I close my Google account?

You will lose access to your HabitForge account. There is no other recovery path — HabitForge has no email-and-password fallback. If you anticipate needing to do this, export your data first.

### Will HabitForge work if Google sign-in is down?

No. Google sign-in is a hard dependency. The app will display a sign-in error and surface a retry button. Once Google services recover, sign-in works again.

### Can I share my account with a family member?

Technically yes (sign them in with your Google account), but the strength meter, streaks, and tracker data will reflect both of you, which usually defeats the purpose. The supported pattern is: each person has their own account.

### Does HabitForge work in incognito / private browsing?

You can sign in, but Firestore's offline cache is not persisted across incognito sessions, so closing the window means an immediate re-sync next time. The app works; it's just slightly slower on a cold open.

### What's the maximum I can store?

There is no hard cap. Practical limits — the speed of Firestore reads — start to be felt around tens of thousands of completion records, but a regular user accumulates that over many years. Export-and-archive is fine if you ever hit that.

### Can I have a HabitForge account without a Google account?

Not currently. See [Sign up — why Google-only](./sign-up#why-google-only).
